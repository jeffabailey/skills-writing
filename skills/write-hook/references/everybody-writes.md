# Principles from *Everybody Writes* applied to hooks

Paraphrased from Ann Handley, *Everybody Writes: Your Go-To Guide to Creating Ridiculously Good Content* (Wiley, 2014). Rule numbers point to the book. Read this when a group of hooks isn't working; SKILL.md already carries the short version.

## The hook is a headline (Rule 69)

- **Spend real time on it.** The headline is not the finishing flourish. It tells the reader what they'll get, how, and why to keep reading.
- **Curiosity gap, in moderation.** Tell enough to make readers curious, not enough to satisfy them. Pushed too far, the gap needs more and more hype and reads as ridiculous. Use it only when it helps the reader decide.
- **Promise what you deliver, specifically.** What does the reader get out of the post? How does it make their work or life better?
- **Put the reader in it.** "14 kinds of pumpkins" offers nothing; "7 pumpkins you can grow on a city balcony" speaks to a person with a balcony. A double benefit is better still.
- **Be economical.** Short enough not to be cut off in feeds and search results (under about 70 characters for a headline).
- **Numbers set expectations.** Odd, specific numbers work. Only use numbers the source supports.
- **Lively words only when true.** If it's surprising, say so. If it isn't, keep quiet.
- **Hype erodes trust.** "Jaw-dropping" marketing speakers, "you won't believe" stories: the reader believes them fine and feels misled. Promising what you can't deliver is bad business.

## Length (Rules 60, 61)

- Social posts and tweets perform best short: about 100-140 characters, and under 120 for a tweet so there's room to share it with a comment or a link.
- Say it again in fewer words. Then again.

## Lead with what matters (Rule 5)

- Put the subject and the action at the start. Move qualifiers to the end or cut them.
- Weak openers to avoid: "According to...", "There is a...", "It is important...", "In my opinion...", "The purpose of this post is...", "I think...".

## Write for the reader (Rules 10, 12, and the Utility x Inspiration x Empathy formula)

- Good writing serves the reader, not the writer. Swap places with them and ask what they'd ask: so what? why me? why now?
- Quality content is useful, inspiring, and empathetic to the reader's problem. The About line should make the usefulness plain.

## Draft badly, then rewrite (Rules 9, 11)

- The first draft gets ideas out; the rewrite makes them good. Humor, the best analogies, and the clearest construction all come on the rewrite.
- For hooks: generate more candidates than you need and keep the best.

## Humor in social writing (Rule 63, Tiffany Beveridge)

- Say it again in fewer words; trust your own voice; use humor when you can, because it's rare and memorable in a noisy feed.
- Tell the most story in the fewest words. Look for the unexpected context: a caption about snow on the floor is mildly funny; calling it Parmesan cheese is the laugh.

## Analogies: familiar yet surprising (Rules 19, 44)

- Frame the unknown with something the reader knows. 842 new accounts means little; "more than the London Eye holds" sticks.
- The comparison must be familiar to this audience and still surprise them. Avoid the obvious ones ("four football fields").

## Cliches (Rule 35)

- A cliche was a fresh image until everyone borrowed it. Business cliches in particular are reflexive and empty: move the needle, at the end of the day, 30,000-foot view, drink from the fire hose, peel back the onion, where the rubber meets the road.
- A well-worn phrase can work, rarely, when it adds charm or a real truth and is used once on purpose.

## Don't preach (Rule 40)

- Openers from a pulpit sound condescending: "Don't forget...", "Never...", "Avoid...", "Remember to...", "Always remember to...". Instruction is fine; dogma is irritating.

## Social posts are dialogue, not pitches (Rule 61)

- Share the why and the who, not just the what. Context and character make a post worth reading.
- Don't pitch-slap: a post that ends in "Order today" is direct marketing, not sharing. Share or solve.

## Hashtags (Rule 62)

- Join conversations people are already having, using terms they already use. People don't want to talk about your brand.
- Personality hashtags can carry voice, sparingly.
- Use no more than two or three hashtags in a single post.

## Tell the truth (Rules 46, 51)

- Don't stretch facts. Check every claim against the source before it goes out.
